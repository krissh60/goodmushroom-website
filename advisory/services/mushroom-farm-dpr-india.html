A detailed project report helps you describe a defined mushroom project: what is being proposed, what assumptions sit behind it, which information is still missing and what should happen next.

When the immediate issue is the site, room flow, utilities or equipment brief, start with the facility consultancy guide. When the decision is whether the project is commercially sensible at all, use the cost and feasibility guide first.

When a DPR is useful

A DPR is useful once you have moved beyond a broad idea. You may know the species, location or intended product format, but need a clear commercial framework that can be discussed with partners, advisers or internal stakeholders.

It is not a shortcut to a loan, subsidy, buyer, licence or profitable result. Those outcomes depend on independent institutions, execution and market conditions. The job of the report is to make the underlying project more legible and testable.

What the DPR actually gives you

The report is a written project framework, not only a page count or a generic template. The precise assumptions and depth depend on the inputs confirmed during scoping.

Project definition

Species, location, facility stage, product format, operating model, intended customer and the decision the report must support.

Technical and workflow frame

Production sequence, room/workflow questions, equipment categories, utilities, hygiene, storage and implementation dependencies relevant to the stated project.

Commercial and financial frame

Capex/opex categories, working-capital questions, route-to-market assumptions, scenario inputs, risks and decision points. Figures remain assumptions to validate.

Action and evidence list

Missing inputs, supplier or site questions, priority actions, boundaries and the next checks needed before investment or submission.

Sample table of contents

  1. Executive summary and the decision the project is intended to support
  2. Project brief: species, location, product route, capacity and operating assumptions
  3. Facility/workflow and equipment categories relevant to the stated model
  4. Procurement, quality, documentation, packaging and buyer-facing requirements
  5. Market route, commercial risks, dependencies and implementation sequence
  6. Capex/opex categories, working-capital questions and financial assumptions
  7. Scenario notes, open questions, missing evidence and recommended next actions

Do not confuse these project documents

Pre-feasibility note

An early decision brief: should the idea be examined further? It is not a detailed implementation or bank-submission document.

Business plan

A broader commercial narrative covering the model, customer, route and assumptions. It may not contain the technical detail a lender or implementer asks for.

DPR / project report

A defined project framework connecting the proposed operation, facility, commercial route, risks and financial assumptions at the agreed depth.

Bank-oriented report or subsidy file

A lender or scheme may require its own format, evidence and professional sign-off. Inclusion is not a promise of approval, funding or subsidy eligibility.

Technical facility plan

A focused layout, workflow, equipment and utility brief. It can be a component of a wider project but is not automatically an engineered drawing set.

Financial model

A calculation framework with input assumptions and scenarios. It is not a guarantee of yield, sales, profitability, repayment or investor return.

What a DPR does not include by default

A DPR does not automatically include buyer introductions, loan or subsidy approval, licence applications, construction supervision, vendor appointment, live operational management or a promise that a projected financial outcome will occur. A requirement outside the fixed scope is discussed and priced in writing first.
